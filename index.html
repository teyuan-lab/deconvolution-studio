How it works

A blurred photo is the sharp scene convolved with a point spread function (PSF), the image a single point of light would make, plus noise. Deblurring needs the PSF first, then a regularised inverse that does not blow up the noise.

Getting the PSF

  • Auto estimate reads the image spectrum: straight dark stripes mean motion blur, rings mean defocus, and a smooth fall-off means Gaussian blur.
  • Blind kernel recovers an arbitrary shape, such as curved camera shake, by alternating between a sharp-edge estimate and a kernel update, from coarse to fine scale.
  • Point sources measures the PSF directly from stars, beads or small bright dots. Straight edge measures it from a sharp high-contrast edge.

Deblurring

  • Total variation keeps edges sharp and removes noise. The image borders are treated as unknown, which avoids ringing.
  • Richardson–Lucy is the standard for astronomy and microscopy. More iterations give a sharper but noisier result.
  • Tikhonov and Wiener are instant closed-form filters.

Regularisation multiplies the automatically chosen weight: move it right for smoother, left for sharper.
